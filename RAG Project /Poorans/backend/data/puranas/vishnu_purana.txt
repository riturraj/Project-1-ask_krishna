THE NATURE OF VISHNU AND CREATION

The Vishnu Purana opens: "I bow to Vishnu, the changeless reality, the one who is beyond beginning and end, the all-pervading, the cause of creation, preservation, and dissolution — who is pure consciousness itself, who is the highest bliss."

Sage Parashara taught: "Vishnu is not a God among other gods. He is the ground from which all gods arise. As rivers arise from the ocean and return to the ocean, all divine powers arise from Vishnu and return to Him. To worship any god sincerely is, at its deepest level, to worship Vishnu — for there is no consciousness anywhere that is not His."

THE STORY OF DHRUVA — WHEN A CHILD SOUGHT GOD ALONE

Young Dhruva, only five years old, was insulted by his stepmother and rejected by his father, King Uttanapada. His mother held him, weeping. He asked her: "Mother, if my father cannot give me the place I deserve, who can?" She replied, half in grief: "Only the Lord of all lords — only Vishnu — can give you what no king on earth can."

The child took this literally and walked into the forest to find God.

Sage Narada found him and tried to dissuade him: "You are too young. God does not appear so easily. Return home." But Dhruva replied: "Sage, you speak of my youth as a limitation. I see it as my qualification. I do not yet know that this is impossible."

Narada taught him the mantra of Vishnu. Dhruva practiced with such intensity — standing on one leg, not eating, barely breathing — that the universe itself shook, and Vishnu appeared before him.

But when Vishnu stood before him in glory, Dhruva found he could not speak. He had prepared so many words in his mind — his demands, his grievances, his rights. But seeing the Lord, all of it fell away. He forgot his original purpose entirely.

Vishnu touched Dhruva's cheek with his conch shell, and in that touch, the child was illumined. He then spoke words of purest praise.

Vishnu said to him: "Dhruva, I know what you came for. I know the wound in your heart. But look at what happened — you came seeking a throne, and in your journey, you found Me. What will you choose now?"

Dhruva replied, with tears: "Lord, what was I thinking? I came looking for a piece of glass and found the diamond of your presence. I no longer want what I came for."

Vishnu smiled and said: "The one who surrenders the lesser desire in my presence receives both the lesser and the greater. You sought a throne — I will give you the pole star, eternal and unmoved, around which all constellations circle. This is the throne that no one can remove you from." (Vishnu Purana, Book 1, Chapter 11-12)

The teaching: Even a five-year-old's anger and grief can become the fuel for the highest spiritual achievement. What matters is not the emotion that begins the journey — but the sincere longing at its core.

THE STORY OF BALI — WHEN THE RIGHTEOUS DEMON SURRENDERED

Bali was a demon king of immense power and even greater virtue. He was more righteous than many of the gods. He gave freely, ruled justly, and his kingdom flourished. When Vishnu came to him in the form of the dwarf Vamana, asking for only three steps of land, Bali's guru Shukracharya warned him: "This is Vishnu in disguise. Do not give what He asks — you will lose everything."

Bali replied: "Guru, I know who He is. I knew the moment He arrived. And knowing this, how could I refuse? If Vishnu Himself desires something from me, what greater honor could there be? My possessions, my kingdom, my power — all of it is already His. I am merely returning what was always His."

Vishnu, moved deeply, said: "Bali, among all who have stood before Me — gods, sages, kings — none has understood generosity as purely as you. You gave knowing you would lose everything. That is not generosity — that is wisdom. That is devotion."

Bali was sent to the netherworld, but Vishnu granted him the rulership of all the nether realms and promised: "I Myself will stand guard at your door, Bali, for as long as you reign. The one who surrenders completely to Me finds that I become his servant." (Vishnu Purana, Book 2)

ON THE NATURE OF MAYA (COSMIC ILLUSION)

Sage Parashara explained to his student Maitreya: "What is Maya? It is not that the world you see is false. The world exists — but you misidentify it. You say 'this is mine' of something that was here before you came and will remain after you go. You say 'I am this body' when you are the awareness that knows the body exists. You say 'I will lose this person' when the soul of that person was never containable within any relationship.

Maya is not a mistake in the world — it is a habit of perception in the mind. And the cure for Maya is not renunciation of the world. The cure is clear seeing — seeing the world exactly as it is, no more, no less. That clear seeing is itself Vishnu's grace." (Vishnu Purana, Book 6)

VISHNU ON KARMA AND FRUIT OF ACTION

The Vishnu Purana records this teaching: "Every soul is responsible for its journey. No one experiences anything they have not, in some form, set in motion. But do not understand karma as punishment. Understand it as a conversation — the universe speaking back to the soul in the language of experience, guiding it, gradually, home.

The one who suffers unjustly — who is righteous but struggling — has accumulated merit that will bear fruit in its season, as surely as a tree bears fruit in its season. The tree does not despair in winter. It knows — in its rootedness — that spring is its nature."

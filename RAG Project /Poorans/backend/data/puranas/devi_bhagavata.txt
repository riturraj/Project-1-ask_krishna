DEVI BHAGAVATA PURANA — SELECTED PASSAGES
(Srimad Devi Bhagavatam — Public Domain Translation)
================================================================================

THE NATURE OF THE SUPREME MOTHER (DEVI)

The Devi Bhagavata Purana declares that the ultimate reality is not male or female, but the active, conscious energy of the universe — represented as the Divine Mother.

"I am the Goddess, the source of all that was, all that is, and all that will be. I am the silence before the word Om, and the silence after it. Even Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva perform their functions of creation, preservation, and dissolution only by My grace, drawing upon My energy (Shakti). Without Me, they are like bodies without breath." (Devi Bhagavata, Book 1)

THE COMPASSION OF THE MOTHER IN TIMES OF DESPAIR

When the gods were utterly defeated by the demon Mahishasura — driven from heaven, stripped of their power, wandering the earth like common beggars — they came together in deep despair. Out of their combined anger and sorrow, a blazing light emerged, which coalesced into the form of the Devi.

She looked at the displaced gods, not with anger at their failure, but with the profound compassion of a mother seeing her children hurt. She said: "Do not fear. Your power was never your own anyway — it was always Mine, lent to you for a season. Mahishasura has defeated your egos, but he cannot defeat the source of your existence. Stand back, My children. What you cannot face, I will consume."

The teaching: When we have exhausted every human effort, when our personal power is entirely spent and we are driven to our knees — that is precisely when the Divine Mother steps forward. Our point of absolute surrender is Her point of entry. We do not have to fight the battles we are not equipped to win. We only have to call Her name. (Devi Bhagavata, Book 5)

DEVI ON SUFFERING AND ILLUSION (MAYA)

King Janamejaya asked the great sage Vyasa: "Why does the Mother, who is all-compassionate, allow her children to suffer in this world of Maya? Why create this painful illusion at all?"

Vyasa replied, transmitting the words of the Devi: "My child, you misunderstand Maya. Maya is not a punishment. It is My play (Leela). If a mother builds a house of blocks for her child to play in, and the child cries when the blocks fall down, is the mother cruel?

"The suffering you feel is the pain of taking the play too seriously. You forget that you are the child, safe in the mother's house. You believe the falling blocks are the end of your world.

"When you cry out from the depths of your illusion, I do not sweep away the blocks. I pick you up. I remind you who you are and where you sit. The moment you remember me, the Maya ceases to bind you, though it continues to dance before your eyes." (Devi Bhagavata, Book 3)

THE SUPREME SURRENDER TO THE MOTHER

In the seventh book, King Himavan asks the Devi for the highest spiritual teaching. She gives him the Devi Gita.

She says: "The highest path is not severe penance, nor vast scriptural knowledge, nor rigid asceticism. The highest path is pure, unmotivated love for Me.

"When a devotee weeps in longing, calling "Mother, Mother!", I drop whatever I am doing in the cosmos and run to him, just as a mother in this world drops her household chores when her infant cries. The infant does not need to know philosophy to summon the mother. The infant only needs to cry.

"Bring Me your brokenness. Bring Me your confusion. Bring Me your failures. I am not a king who demands your perfection — I am a Mother who wants only your presence. Rest your head in My lap. Let the world spin as it will. You are safe." (Devi Bhagavata, Devi Gita)
